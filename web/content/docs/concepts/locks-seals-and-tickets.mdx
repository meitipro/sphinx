---
title: Locks, seals and tickets
description: How the answer is held before play, why nobody can copy a sealed answer, and what a ticket is.
---

## The lock

The host's answer and notes are hashed with a random salt in the browser:

```text
lock = sha256(answer + "|" + notes + "|" + salt)
```

Only the lock goes on chain with the riddle. Nobody can read the answer from it, and the host cannot change it: the reveal
is checked by recomputing the hash, and any other answer, notes or salt is refused. The host cannot reveal while sealing is
open, only in the reveal window after it.

A bar is refused in the answer and in the salt. That keeps one reading of every lock: a host cannot lock answer `piano`
with notes `keyboard|anything` and later claim the answer was `piano|keyboard`.

## The seal

A player's answer is hashed with their own salt and their address:

```text
seal = sha256(answer + "|" + salt + "|" + lower_case(address))
```

While sealing is open, only seals are public. Because the address is inside the hash, copying another player's seal buys
nothing: the copier's reveal is hashed with the copier's address and never matches. One seal per address per riddle, with
the exact entry fee.

## The ticket

A ticket is the answer, the notes (for a host) and the salt behind a lock or a seal. The site saves every ticket in your
browser before the transaction that needs it is signed, and offers it as a JSON download. The [player page](/me) lists
them and can load a ticket file back.

<Callout type="warn">Lose the ticket and you lose the salt. A sealed answer that is never opened is `UNREVEALED`: it is never judged and cannot win. Its fee goes to the winners, or comes back if nobody is correct.</Callout>

## Same bytes everywhere

The contract hashes with Python's `hashlib`, the browser with SubtleCrypto. Both are tested against the same vectors.
See [Hashing, with test vectors](/docs/reference/hashing).
