---
title: Writing notes on what counts
description: The notes decide every edge case. How to write them so validators agree and players get the answer they earned.
---

The prompt tells the validators: *the notes decide edge cases*. The notes are locked with the answer before anyone plays,
so they are the host's ruling on every near miss, made before seeing a single guess.

## Say it in the words the prompt asks in

The judge labels `CORRECT` (names the same thing), `CLOSE` (right direction, not the same thing, or too broad) and `WRONG`
(anything else, or more than one guess). Notes that use those words leave validators nothing to guess:

> Any computer keyboard, in any language. A piano is close: keys, but no space or enter. Two guesses in one answer are wrong.

- **Name what counts.** "Any computer keyboard, in any language."
- **Name the near misses you expect, and call them close.** "A piano is close."
- **Name what is wrong.** "February is wrong."
- **Say how numbers may be written.** "Any way of writing 12."

## Keep it about meaning

Spelling, articles, synonyms and other languages are already fine unless the notes say otherwise. Write notes only for
what the rubric cannot know: which near misses are close, which are wrong, and whether a broader answer counts.

## Try them first

On [Host a riddle](/new), **Try the notes** shows how up to three sample answers would be labelled by one model with the
contract's prompt. It is a preview only; validators decide on chain. If a sample you think is correct comes back `CLOSE`,
say so in the notes.

## What notes cannot do

Notes cannot change money: winners, shares, fees and refunds are contract code, and the prompt never mentions them. Notes
cannot make a second guess count, and an instruction inside a player's answer is ignored.
