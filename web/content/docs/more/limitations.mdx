---
title: Limitations
description: What Sphinx does not do, and what it cannot know.
---

Sphinx judges whether an answer means the same as the host's, as the notes define it. It does not judge whether the riddle
is fair, and some things are out of its reach on purpose.

## Wallets are not people

One seal per address, and each address pays the entry fee. A solver with several wallets can seal the right answer from
each of them. Fees and small First N sizes keep that in proportion; one seal per verified person with GenLayer Identity is a
stretch goal, not built.

## A host can tell a friend

Nothing stops a host from sharing the answer with someone before the riddle closes. The host's record page shows every
riddle they hosted and who won, which is the only defence.

## Trivia is open book

Nobody can see what players look up. Hosts should write riddles, not lookups.

## Answers are public after the reveal

Every answer is shown next to the address that sealed it, once revealed. Sphinx is not anonymous.

## Near misses are the leader's

Validators agree on which answers are `CORRECT`. Whether a wrong answer is shown as `CLOSE` or `WRONG` is the leader's
label, stored for display. Both pay the same, nothing.

## The shortest windows are one hour

Sealing and reveal each last at least one hour, so a riddle takes at least two hours from post to judging. To try the whole
loop yourself, host a riddle with one-hour windows and seal from a second wallet.

## Model judgments can differ

A batch where the validators' models disagree on which answers are correct stores nothing; anyone can send `judge` again.
The golden cases on [Evaluation results](/docs/more/evaluation) show how the judge behaved on chain, misses included.

## Test network

Everything runs on GenLayer Studio Next with test GEN. Previews of the notes need a model key on the site's server and are
off when none is set.
